\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

The method makes three claims and we test them in order. \textbf{(i)} In a two-code
video autoencoder with a free decoder, the static code is close to unused, and no
independence penalty repairs that. \textbf{(ii)} A base-plus-zero-mean-residual decoder
makes the static code load-bearing, and this shows up on a measure that an entangled
control fails. \textbf{(iii)} Once the redundancy is removed the same model runs at
$24\times$ compression, where it is at parity with strong video encoders. We then report
what the design costs, and one negative result about how factorization is usually
evaluated.

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}
\textbf{Data.} \emph{CLEVRER} \citep{clevrer} (synthetic, fixed camera, ground-truth
attributes and per-object positions) and \emph{Something-Something-v2} \citep{ssv2}
(real, handheld, $174$ motion-defined actions). CLEVRER chunks are $W{=}33$ frames
$\to$ Wan-2.2 latent $(48,9,8,8)$; SSv2 chunks are $W{=}17 \to (48,5,8,8)$. The full
SSv2 run uses all $193{,}690$ clips ($163{,}717$ train / $28{,}891$ val after windowing),
Wan-encoded to $770{,}432$ chunks. \textbf{Protocol.} The VAE is frozen throughout. We
report mean$\pm$std over three seeds for every headline number and mark single-seed
results explicitly. \textbf{Configuration.} Unless noted, \method{} is
$\zs{=}576$ floats ($9$ channels on $8\times8$) and $\zd{=}64$ floats per frame
($1$ channel on $8\times8$): $1{,}152$ floats per chunk, $24\times$ compression.

\subsection{Q1: Does the static code carry anything?}
\label{sec:q1}
We measure this by ablation --- zero one code and re-decode. A code that matters cannot
be removed cheaply. \Cref{tab:ablate} is the paper's central table.

Three readings. First, the failure is real and large: with a free decoder the static
code costs $11\%$ to delete against the dynamics code's $714\%$. Second, the structural
decoder inverts the ratio --- $484\%$ against $149\%$ --- so both codes become necessary
and neither is sufficient. Third, the effect is the \emph{decoder's}: at the same rate,
removing the constraint returns the static code to $60\%$. Capacity is not the lever;
in a separate measurement, giving the static code eight times the rate under a free
decoder moved its solo reconstruction from $24.90$ to $24.89$\,dB and left $76\%$ of
its dimensions unused.

\subsection{Q2: Is the split real, and does the usual evidence show it?}
\label{sec:q2}
Ablation says both codes are needed; it does not say they hold \emph{different} things.
For that we measure code overlap directly with kernel CKA \citep{cka}, and we include an
\textbf{entangled control} --- one shared convolutional trunk feeding both heads, an
encoder that cannot route the factors apart. It should fail any valid test.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering\small
\caption{\textbf{Q2 --- overlap between the codes, and a control that must fail}
(CLEVRER, $1{,}152$ floats). Kernel CKA $\downarrow$ measures shared information. The
independence penalty $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{indep}}$ is the quantity such objectives
optimise. \textbf{The identity/motion swap test is passed equally by the entangled
control}, so it does not separate a factorizing encoder from one that cannot factorize;
CKA does.}
\label{tab:overlap}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Model & CKA $\downarrow$ & $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{indep}}$ & swap margin \\
\midrule
\textbf{\method{}} [3] & $\mathbf{0.324}${\tiny$\pm.003$} & $0.0042$ & $+0.053${\tiny$\pm.013$} \\
Entangled encoder (shared trunk) & $0.376$ & $0.0051$ & $+0.054$ \\
No constraint (free decoder) & $0.398$ & $0.0048$ & $-0.071$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\method{} has lower overlap than both controls, and the ordering is the one a working
factorization predicts. Two negatives matter as much as the positive.
\textbf{The independence penalty does not track overlap}: it reads $0.0042$--$0.0051$
across models whose CKA spans $0.324$--$0.398$, and it ranks them backwards. And
\textbf{the swap test is confounded}: the entangled control scores $+0.054$ against
\method{}'s $+0.053\pm0.013$, reproduced at a second rate. The swap measures whether the
\emph{decoder} routes identity through the static code --- which our decoder guarantees
by construction --- not whether the \emph{encoder} separated anything. We report this
because the test is standard in this literature \citep{s3vae,cdsvae,divid} and, without
this control, a positive result is not evidence. We qualify the CKA claim in
\cref{tab:ssv2diag}: on CLEVRER it orders the models correctly, but on real video it does
not, and we no longer claim CKA as a sufficient test.

\subsection{Q3: Reconstruction against strong encoders, at matched rate}
\label{sec:q3}
Every representation is frozen and mapped back to the Wan latent by an
\emph{identical-capacity} head, then decoded to pixels by the frozen VAE. This is the
only setting in which our number and the baselines' are produced the same way.

not a win.

CLEVRER is synthetic and its scenes are near-identical across clips, so we repeat the
protocol unchanged on real video. Ground truth here is the \emph{source} clip rather
than the VAE's own round trip, which lets us separate two different losses: what the
frozen substrate costs, and what our code costs on top of it.

better than VideoMAE, which says that on this substrate the pooled self-supervised
features retain little that a channel mean does not already carry. These are
single-seed.

\subsection{Q3c: The same controls on real video --- where CKA stops working}
\label{sec:ssv2diag}
\cref{tab:overlap} ran these diagnostics on CLEVRER. Repeating them on SSv2, against an
entangled control trained on all $163{,}717$ clips with the committed configuration and a
shared trunk, changes one of the conclusions.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering\small
\caption{\textbf{Q3c --- overlap, ablation and reconstruction on SSv2} (three seeds for
\method{}; $400$ val videos for ablation, $600$ for CKA). Deletion cost is the \%
increase in reconstruction MSE when a code is zeroed. \textbf{Linear CKA ranks the
entangled control as the \emph{better}-factorized model}, and the deletion costs do not
separate the two either. What does separate them is the dynamics code: the control's is
collapsed (effective rank $69$ of $320$ against our $117$), it is $13$ points cheaper to
delete, and it reads actions far worse (\cref{tab:libero}).}
\label{tab:ssv2diag}
\begin{tabular}{lccccc}
\toprule
& CKA lin.\ $\downarrow$ & CKA rbf $\downarrow$ & del.\ $\zs$ & del.\ $\zd$ & PSNR $\uparrow$ \\
\midrule
\textbf{\method{}} & $0.182${\tiny$\pm.021$} & $\mathbf{0.289}${\tiny$\pm.018$}
  & $332\%${\tiny$\pm9$} & $\mathbf{48\%}${\tiny$\pm1$} & $\mathbf{15.05}${\tiny$\pm.07$} \\
Entangled (shared trunk) & $\mathbf{0.022}$ & $0.300$ & $351\%$ & $35\%$ & $14.94$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\textbf{A negative result about our own metric.} On CLEVRER we reported CKA as the
diagnostic that separates a factorizing encoder from one that cannot factorize. On SSv2
it does not: linear CKA reads $0.022$ for the entangled control against our $0.182$, and
the RBF variant reads $0.300$ against $0.289$ --- indistinguishable. The control's low
linear CKA is not evidence of a better split; its dynamics code has collapsed to an
effective rank of $69$ of $320$ dimensions (ours: $117$), and two codes overlap less when
one of them carries less. Deletion cost fails here too, and for a reason we can state
exactly: the base+delta wiring makes the time-constant output a function of $\zs$ by
construction, so a \emph{large} deletion cost for $\zs$ is guaranteed by the architecture and is
available to the control for free. We therefore retract the general claim that CKA
verifies the split. The measurements that do separate the two models on real video are
the dynamics code's deletion cost ($48\%$ vs $35\%$), its effective rank, and its
action read-out --- all properties of $\zd$, none of them an overlap statistic.

\subsection{Q4: Does it transfer to real video at scale?}
\label{sec:q4}
We train the same configuration on all of SSv2 ($163{,}717$ clips) and probe the frozen
codes for the action class --- a \emph{motion} task, on which a working split should
favour $\zd$.

The dynamics code reaches $10.32\%$ at $320$ floats against the full $15{,}360$-float
latent's $13.70\%$ --- $75\%$ of what the substrate affords, at $48\times$ fewer numbers.
\emph{A correction to our own prior work:} we previously reported this ceiling as
$9.5\%$ and argued from it that SSv2 accuracy is input-capped. That figure was measured
on an $8$k-clip subset and was underfit; a linear probe on $15{,}360$ dimensions needs
far more data. The properly-fit ceiling is $13.70\%$, so the gap to fully-supervised
video encoders is smaller than we claimed, and the ``input-capped'' argument should not
be relied on.

\subsection{Q4b: Reading actions --- the asymmetry is produced by training}
\label{sec:libero}
SSv2 action classes are coarse. LIBERO gives a continuous, physically-grounded target:
the $6$-DoF end-effector command and the binary gripper state. We make the target
\emph{window-local} --- for the chunk spanning frames $[s,s{+}W)$ the label is the action
commanded over exactly those frames --- which is what sharpens the test. There is a
single $\zs$ for the whole demonstration, so it cannot know \emph{which} window it is
being asked about; if it still matched $\zd$, the codes would not be split. Whole tasks
are held out ($15$ of $90$), so no probe sees its test task's action statistics.

Trained, $\zd$ reads actions at $R^2\,0.662$ against $\zs$'s $0.485$ --- a gap of
$0.177$, against the entangled control's $0.061$ ($0.564$ vs $0.503$) --- and adding

the mean-pooled raw latent reaches $R^2\,0.591$ at $48$ floats, above our static code and
below our dynamics code; on this task the factorized codes bracket the trivial baseline
rather than dominating it.

\subsection{Q6: What the design costs}
\label{sec:q5}
Two costs, both real. \textbf{Absolute fidelity.} At matched settings \method{}
reconstructs at $32.29\pm0.08$\,dB against $33.00$\,dB for an unconstrained model at the
same rate and $34.63$\,dB for the prior configuration at $2{,}400$ floats (frozen-VAE
ceiling $46.85$\,dB). The structure costs $0.71$\,dB at $24\times$; notably this cost
\emph{shrinks} with compression --- it is $4.03$\,dB at $2.8\times$ --- because at low
rate the dynamics code could not absorb the scene in any case.
\textbf{Semantic accuracy is bounded by the substrate.} CLEVRER attribute mAP is
$0.725\pm0.008$ against $0.749\pm0.005$ for the prior configuration; across more than
eighty configurations spanning rate, code shape, memory, auxiliary teachers and loss
weights, this quantity never left $0.72$--$0.77$. It is a property of the frozen latent,
not of the encoder.

\label{sec:q6}

In the rate-matched block, the structural decoder is what produces the factorization:
removing it returns the static code to $60\%$ at identical rate, and it is the only
single change that does so. Separate trunks buy $0.0023$ reconstruction and $91$ points
of ablation cost over an entangled encoder, but --- per \cref{tab:overlap} --- their
effect is visible in CKA and not in the swap test.

The loss-term block is weaker evidence and we treat it as such. The consistency term is
the sole source of semantic content: removing it costs $0.06$ mAP while \emph{improving}
reconstruction, the clearest statement of that trade we have. The independence penalty
run improves reconstruction and leaves the static code at $11\%$ --- but that run also
differs in rate and decoder, so it shows the penalty does not \emph{rescue} a free
decoder rather than isolating the penalty itself. Rate-matched versions of these three
rows are the main outstanding experiment.

\subsection{Status}
\label{sec:plan}
Confirmed over three seeds: \cref{tab:ablate}, \cref{tab:overlap} (\method{} row),
\cref{tab:ssv2}, and the PSNR figures in \cref{sec:q5}. Single-seed and to be repeated
before camera-ready: the controls in \cref{tab:ablate,tab:ablations} and all of
\cref{tab:matched} --- where the top three rows are close enough that seeds decide the
ordering. Not re-run in this revision: the DROID moving-camera study
(\cref{sec:spatial-dyn}), which is reported from the earlier model version.
