\paragraph{Self-supervised video representation.}
Masked and predictive video encoders --- VideoMAE \citep{videomae}, V-JEPA
\citep{vjepa}, and image-level DINOv2 \citep{dinov2} --- learn strong transferable
features that are evaluated by frozen-feature probes (linear, attentive-pool,
$k$-NN) and label efficiency on Kinetics and Something-Something. They produce a
single unstructured feature grid and are trained on human-action video; they do
not expose a named identity/dynamics/event factorization, nor are they aimed at
object-physics or robot data. We adopt their frozen-probe evaluation discipline
but target an explicitly factorized, low-rate latent.

\paragraph{Factorized and disentangled video.}
A line of sequential VAEs splits video into a time-invariant content code and a
per-frame motion code: DSVAE \citep{dsvae}, S3VAE \citep{s3vae}, C-DSVAE
\citep{cdsvae}, MoCoGAN \citep{mocogan}; VidTwin \citep{vidtwin} carries this into
a modern video VAE with separate structure and dynamics latents, and object-centric
SlotFormer \citep{slotformer} validates its factorization by downstream physical
reasoning. Three properties are near-universal in this family and matter for us: the
disentanglement is demonstrated almost only on synthetic or narrow data, the
\emph{motion} code is a global per-frame vector, and --- most importantly --- each
method obtains its factorization by \emph{training a VAE from scratch}. Our analysis
explains why the global motion code caps real-video reconstruction; our
spatial-dynamics code is the fix, and unlike this literature we demonstrate the split
on \emph{real} moving-camera video and, crucially, \emph{on top of an already-trained
frozen video VAE} rather than by training a new one.

\paragraph{Video tokenizers and world models.}
Tokenizers --- MAGVIT-v2 \citep{magvitv2}, Cosmos \citep{cosmos}, PVDM
\citep{pvdm} --- and latent world models --- DreamerV3 \citep{dreamerv3}, TD-MPC2
\citep{tdmpc2}, iVideoGPT \citep{ivideogpt}, Genie \citep{genie} --- all keep a
per-frame \emph{spatial} token grid and are judged by generation quality
(FVD/PSNR) and, for world models, by planning return and sample efficiency. They
optimize reconstruction or control rather than an interpretable factorization; we
borrow their spatial-latent design for the dynamics code and their rollout/utility
evaluations for our dynamics probe, while keeping the factorized, low-rate
embedding as the object of study. Most directly related, PV-VAE \citep{pvvae}
enriches a video VAE's latent with a \emph{predictive} reconstruction objective and
reports improved motion-awareness and downstream understanding --- but its latent
stays a single \emph{entangled} code and, like the tokenizers above, it is obtained
by \emph{training the VAE}. We share the predictive ingredient (our
forward-prediction term) yet differ on both axes that matter here: we \emph{factorize}
the latent into named, controllable slots, and we do so on a \emph{frozen} VAE
without retraining it.

\paragraph{Camera- and viewpoint-aware representation.}
Geometry-aware attention (GTA \citep{gta}) and projective positional encodings
(PRoPE \citep{prope}) inject known camera pose to improve novel-view synthesis and
cross-view reasoning; MosaicMem \citep{mosaicmem} combines explicit 3D lifting with
implicit dynamics for camera-consistent memory; view-invariant policies and world
models \citep{reviwo,vista} demonstrate viewpoint robustness by held-out-camera
control. Our camera path uses known pose in the same spirit, but as an add-on to a
factorized embedding rather than for synthesis, and we test camera-invariance of
the \emph{state} directly via a pose-readout asymmetry.

\paragraph{Visual representations for robotics.}
R3M \citep{r3m}, VIP \citep{vip}, MVP \citep{mvp}, and Voltron \citep{voltron}
learn frozen visual encoders judged by behavior-cloning success rate, action/state
probes, and sample efficiency on manipulation. We evaluate \method{}'s dynamics
code with the same frozen-encoder protocol on LIBERO \citep{libero}, and use these
methods as the baselines for downstream usefulness.

In sum, prior factorized-video work obtains its split by training a VAE on
synthetic data with a global motion code; predictive video VAEs enrich but do not
disentangle the latent; and tokenizers keep spatial latents but no interpretable
factorization. \method{} is, to our knowledge, the first to recover a named
identity/dynamics/camera factorization \emph{from a frozen, pretrained video VAE
without retraining it}, at high compression and on real moving-camera video, once
the dynamics code is made spatial.
