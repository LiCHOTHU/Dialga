\paragraph{Factorized and disentangled video.}
A long line of sequential VAEs splits video into a time-invariant content code and a
per-frame motion code: DSVAE \citep{dsvae}, S3VAE \citep{s3vae}, C-DSVAE \citep{cdsvae},
MoCoGAN \citep{mocogan}; VidTwin \citep{vidtwin} carries this into a modern video VAE
with separate structure and dynamics latents, and DeCo-VAE \citep{decovae} decomposes
into keyframe, motion and residual with dedicated encoders and a shared 3D decoder.
DiViD \citep{divid} moves the idea to diffusion, extracting a global static token from
the first frame and per-frame dynamics as \emph{residuals} against it. Two properties
are near-universal in this family and matter here. First, every method obtains its
factorization by \emph{training a generative model from scratch}, where we adapt a
frozen VAE. Second, and more consequentially, they enforce the split with an
\emph{independence-style objective} --- an orthogonality penalty in DiViD, mutual-
information or adversarial terms elsewhere. Our central negative result
(\cref{sec:indep}) is that such penalties are satisfied by a static code carrying pure
noise, and that in our setting the penalty is fully satisfied by a model whose static
code is nearly unused. We enforce the split in the decoder instead.

\paragraph{Evaluating disentanglement.}
This literature evaluates by cross-leakage probes and by swap accuracy: encode two
clips, cross their codes, and check which donor the hybrid resembles \citep{s3vae,
cdsvae,divid}. We report in \cref{sec:q2} that an encoder with a \emph{shared} trunk ---
one that cannot route the factors apart --- passes the swap test as well as ours, at two
different rates. The test measures the decoder's routing rather than the encoder's
split. We therefore report code overlap with kernel CKA \citep{cka}, which does separate
the two, and recommend the shared-trunk control accompany any swap result.

\paragraph{Self-supervised video representation.}
Masked and predictive video encoders --- VideoMAE \citep{videomae}, V-JEPA
\citep{vjepa}, image-level DINOv2 \citep{dinov2} --- learn strong transferable features
evaluated by frozen-feature probes. They produce a single unstructured feature grid and
expose no named factorization. We adopt their frozen-probe discipline, and compare
against them under a matched decode protocol (\cref{sec:q3}); we note there that their
commonly-quoted compression figures are a property of the mean-pooling applied by the
probe, since their native token grids are larger than the VAE latent itself.

\paragraph{Video tokenizers and world models.}
Tokenizers --- MAGVIT-v2 \citep{magvitv2}, Cosmos \citep{cosmos}, PVDM \citep{pvdm},
VideoFlexTok \citep{videoflextok} --- and latent world models \citep{dreamerv3,tdmpc2,
ivideogpt,genie} keep a per-frame spatial token grid and are judged by generation
quality and planning return rather than by an interpretable split. We borrow their
spatial-latent design for the dynamics code (\cref{sec:spatial-dyn}) and compare against
VideoFlexTok at its own rate. Most directly related, PV-VAE \citep{pvvae} enriches a
video VAE with a predictive objective but leaves the latent entangled, and like the
tokenizers is obtained by training the VAE.

\paragraph{Static and dynamic memory.}
Outside the tokenizer setting, several systems separate persistent scene structure from
transient motion for reconstruction and generation: MosaicMem \citep{mosaicmem} composes
spatially-aligned memory patches into a queried view, and Mem4D \citep{mem4d} keeps a
persistent structure memory alongside a transient dynamics memory, naming the same
tension we measure --- one representation cannot simultaneously hold long-term static
structure and high-fidelity dynamics. Our result is the compressed-code analogue: the
tension is real, and it is resolved by the decoder's wiring rather than by a loss.

\paragraph{Camera- and viewpoint-aware representation.}
Geometry-aware attention \citep{gta} and projective positional encodings \citep{prope}
inject known camera pose for synthesis and cross-view reasoning; view-invariant policies
\citep{reviwo,vista} demonstrate viewpoint robustness. Our moving-camera study
(\cref{sec:droid}) uses known pose in this spirit, as a component of the dynamics-code
analysis rather than as a headline claim.

In sum, prior factorized-video work trains a generative model from scratch and enforces
its split with an independence objective, evaluated by swap accuracy. We adapt a frozen
VAE, show that the independence objective cannot enforce what it is asked to and that
swap accuracy does not verify it, and obtain the split structurally instead.
