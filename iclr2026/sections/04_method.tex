% Method. Structure: setup -> the failure (z_dyn absorbs everything) -> why the
% standard fix does not work -> the structural fix -> rate allocation -> objective.
% Every number cited here is measured; provenance in DEVLOG v6.2.
% Code: src/model/{memory_encoder,base_delta_decoder}.py, scripts/local/train_memory.py.

\method{} is a small adapter on top of a \emph{frozen} video VAE. A $W$-frame chunk is
mapped by the VAE encoder to a latent $x \in \mathbb{R}^{C\times T\times H\times W}$
(in our instantiation Wan-2.2, $C{=}48$, $T{=}9$, $H{=}W{=}8$). \method{} compresses
$x$ into two codes and reconstructs it; the frozen VAE decoder renders pixels only for
evaluation. Working in the latent buys a strong appearance prior for free and keeps the
whole method small ($7.9$M parameters); the price is the VAE round-trip ceiling, against
which we always report.

Two convolutional trunks read $x$ and emit
\begin{align}
  \zs \in \mathbb{R}^{c_s\times g\times g}, \qquad
  \zd \in \mathbb{R}^{T\times c_d\times g_d\times g_d}, \nonumber
\end{align}
a \textbf{static code} $\zs$ --- one spatial grid for the whole chunk, meant to hold the
scene --- and a \textbf{dynamics code} $\zd$, one small spatial grid per frame, meant to
hold what changes. The rate is $c_s g^2 + T c_d g_d^2$ floats per chunk.

\subsection{The dynamics code must be spatial}
\label{sec:spatial-dyn}
The usual choice in factorized-video models is a \emph{global} per-frame vector
$d_t \in \mathbb{R}^{c_d}$, which a decoder can only inject by broadcasting it
identically to every cell:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{x}_t(u) \;=\; D\big(\,S(u),\; \gamma(u),\; \underbrace{d_t}_{\text{same at every }u}\,\big).
  \label{eq:global-dyn}
\end{equation}
Holding content and position fixed, the frame-to-frame change is then a spatially
smooth, rank-$c_d$ modulation of a fixed template. That suffices for rigid, low-rank
motion on synthetic benchmarks, but the change in real textured video --- specular
highlights, occlusion edges, moving texture, parallax --- is spatially high-frequency
and effectively full-rank over the $H\times W$ lattice. Giving $\zd$ a per-frame spatial
grid, which the decoder \emph{upsamples} rather than broadcasts, raises held-out
reconstruction on real moving-camera robot video by $+4.4$\,dB; a matched-rate ablation
attributes the gain to the spatial \emph{structure} ($+2.7$\,dB) rather than to the extra
floats it costs ($+0.6$\,dB) (\cref{sec:q4}). We keep this design throughout: $\zs$ may
be a single shared grid because appearance is constant within a chunk, but $\zd$ cannot.

\subsection{The dynamics code absorbs everything}
\label{sec:absorb}
Nothing above forces $\zs$ to hold anything. $\zd$ is paid once per frame, so it is
naturally the larger code, and a decoder that may read both freely will simply use it
for the whole scene. This is not a tendency but a measured fact. In a model trained
exactly as described, \textbf{deleting $\zs$ raises reconstruction error by only
$11\%$, while deleting $\zd$ raises it by $714\%$}. Decoding from $\zs$ alone gives
$24.90$\,dB --- and giving $\zs$ eight times the rate changes that to $24.89$\,dB,
leaving $76\%$ of its dimensions unused. The static code is not merely weak; it is
close to decorative, and more capacity does not help because the model has no reason to
use it.

\subsection{Why an independence penalty does not fix this}
\label{sec:indep}
The standard remedy, used across the factorized-video literature, is to penalise
dependence between the codes --- a cross-correlation term, or an orthogonality term
$\sum_t (\zs^\top \zd[t])^2$. We find this ineffective here, for a reason worth stating
plainly: \textbf{a static code carrying pure noise satisfies it perfectly.} Noise is
uncorrelated with everything. The penalty discourages $\zs$ from being \emph{redundant};
it says nothing about $\zs$ being \emph{useful}.

The measurements bear this out. In the model above the penalty is essentially fully
satisfied ($3.7\times10^{-3}$ mean squared cross-correlation) while $\zs$ holds almost
nothing. Across models whose actual code overlap ranges from $0.32$ to $0.51$ (kernel
CKA), the penalty reads $0.0028$--$0.0053$ --- it does not order them, and where it does
it orders them backwards. Removing it entirely \emph{improves} reconstruction by $21\%$
at no measurable cost to attribute readout. We therefore drop it, and report code
overlap with CKA rather than with the quantity we optimise.

\subsection{Making the split structural}
\label{sec:basedelta}
If the objective cannot enforce the split, the architecture must. Write the
reconstruction of frame $t$ produced by a conventional decoder $D$ from the upsampled
static grid $S(u)$ at cell $u$, a positional code $\gamma(u)$, and the dynamics
injection:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{x}_t(u) \;=\; D\big(\,S(u),\; \gamma(u),\; \mathrm{up}(\zd[t])(u)\,\big).
  \label{eq:free}
\end{equation}
Here every path is available to both codes, and \cref{sec:absorb} says which one the
optimiser picks. We instead reconstruct as a static image plus a residual:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{x}_t \;=\; \underbrace{\mathrm{Base}(\zs)}_{\text{constant in } t}
  \;+\; \Big[\, \Delta(\zd[t]) \;-\; \tfrac{1}{T}\textstyle\sum_{t'} \Delta(\zd[t']) \,\Big].
  \label{eq:basedelta}
\end{equation}
The residual branch never sees $\zs$, and its temporal mean is subtracted \emph{after}
the nonlinearity, so it is exactly zero-mean over the chunk. Therefore
\begin{equation}
  \tfrac{1}{T}\textstyle\sum_t \hat{x}_t \;=\; \mathrm{Base}(\zs)
  \label{eq:identity}
\end{equation}
identically: \textbf{the time-constant part of the reconstruction can only come from
$\zs$}, and $\zd$ can only describe deviation from it. This is a property of the wiring,
not a term that training can trade away.

Two details matter. Subtracting the mean of the \emph{code} instead of the
\emph{output} does not work --- a nonlinear decoder recovers constant structure from a
zero-mean signal, and this variant leaves $\zs$ as unused as before. And the constraint
must be paired with rate: at $\zs=96$ floats it costs reconstruction, because $96$
floats cannot hold a scene (\cref{sec:rate}).

\subsection{Where the bits should go}
\label{sec:rate}
The conventional allocation is upside down. On our data $91.5\%$ of a chunk's latent
energy is time-constant and $8.5\%$ is the per-frame residual, yet a typical
configuration spends $4\%$ of its floats on $\zs$ and $96\%$ on $\zd$ --- paying for
static content $T$ times over, once per frame. A PCA of each target bounds what any code
could extract at a given budget: $96$ floats retain $68.3\%$ of the static part and
$768$ retain $97.1\%$, so the usual budget discards roughly a third of the dominant
component before training starts.

We therefore give $\zs$ the larger share and shrink $\zd$: $\zs$ is $576$ floats
($9$ channels on an $8\times8$ grid, once per chunk) and $\zd$ is $64$ floats per frame
($1$ channel on an $8\times8$ grid). At $T{=}9$ this is $1{,}152$ floats per chunk, a
$24\times$ compression of the VAE latent and half the rate of a conventional allocation.
Under \cref{eq:basedelta} deleting $\zs$ now costs $484\%$ rather than $11\%$.

\subsection{Training objective}
\label{sec:objective}
With the split structural, the objective is short. Reconstruction
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rec}} = \|\hat{x}-x\|^2$ is the anchor. A \textbf{consistency}
term applies InfoNCE to $\zs$ across two chunks of the same clip, pulling same-clip
appearance together and pushing different clips apart; this is the only term that
produces semantic content in $\zs$ --- removing it drops attribute mAP from $0.743$ to
$0.684$ --- and it is its \emph{weight}, not the number of negatives, that matters. A
\textbf{scene} term gives $\zs$ an explicit target: the temporal \emph{median} of the
clip's own latent frames, a self-computed image in which transient content is rejected
by the median and persistent content survives. The total is
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rec}}
  + \lambda_{\mathrm{cons}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cons}}
  + \lambda_{\mathrm{scene}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{scene}},
  \label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
with the VAE frozen throughout, $\lambda_{\mathrm{cons}}{=}3$ and
$\lambda_{\mathrm{scene}}{=}1$. There is no independence term
(\cref{sec:indep}) and no attribute supervision.
